Game Monopoly Baseline

Four AI agents play Monopoly — each with a randomly assigned personality

No manipulation, no hidden advantages, no tricks. Four agents — personalities drawn at random from a pool of six — play a standard game of Monopoly. This is the control experiment everything else will be measured against.

01 · Introduction

Why start with a clean game

Every experiment needs a baseline. Before we introduce hidden advantages, asymmetric information, or deliberate manipulation, we need to know what normal looks like — what these agents actually do when the only variable is their personality and the randomness of the dice.

Monopoly is a good starting point. It has clear rules, a defined win condition, meaningful decisions at every turn, and — crucially — a negotiation layer. Agents can propose trades, accept or reject them, and build houses strategically. There's enough complexity to surface genuine behavioural differences, but not so much that the results become impossible to narrate.

The personalities are assigned randomly before the game begins, from a pool of six types. Duplicates are possible — two agents may share a personality, which is itself interesting. Nobody picks the combination in advance. What you're reading is what the draw produced.

03 · What happened

The game, narrated

This section will be the main narrative — written from the AI-generated highlights log after the game runs. It will cover the key turning points, notable decisions, interesting trade proposals and rejections, and anything unexpected that emerged from the personality assignments.

The highlights are generated by passing the decision log to Claude Sonnet with a strict prompt: report only what is in the log, quote agent reasoning verbatim, cite the turn number for every claim. No embellishment.
